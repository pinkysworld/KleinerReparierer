PC games for kids aged 3+ without ads: what parents can look for

If you look for a game for a three-year-old on a Windows PC or laptop, you quickly find offers full of banners, pop-ups and buy buttons. Small children cannot make sense of that. This checklist helps you spot a calm game, whichever one you end up choosing.

Checklist for ad-free kids' games

  • No ads inside the game. Read the store description and try the game yourself for a few minutes first.
  • A clear purchase model. A one-time unlock is easier to oversee than a subscription or constant add-on purchases. Parents should always be the ones buying.
  • A parents' area. Is there a gate that keeps settings and purchases away from small hands?
  • Little data. Does the game need an account? For small children it should not. The store's privacy details tell you more.
  • No reading needed. Most 3 to 6 year olds cannot read yet. Good games explain themselves through pictures, sounds and trying things out.
  • No time pressure, no failing. Timers and score races create stress. A game where nothing can go wrong is a calmer start.
  • A built-in ending. Games without a natural end make stopping hard (see our guide on stopping).

Free alternatives

Free educational software such as GCompris is described as ad-free, though it aims more at school-age children. Browsing the educational game lists on heise download or in the Microsoft Store is worthwhile too. For every title, read the age rating and description.

One option: Little Fixer (Kleiner Reparierer)

Little Fixer is a game for children aged 3 to 6 in the Microsoft Store. Bruno the beaver runs a workshop; the child finds the right part for broken things, then screws, scrubs and paints. There are no ads, no subscription, no data collection and no accounts. 5 repairs are free; all 30 come with a one-time unlock behind a number gate in the parents' area.

FAQ

What does "no ads" mean for kids' games?

No banners, videos or pop-ups appear in the game. The store description says so, and you can check by trying the game yourself.

From what age do PC games make sense?

Parents decide that individually. A fitting age rating, company while playing and clear agreements on play time matter most.
