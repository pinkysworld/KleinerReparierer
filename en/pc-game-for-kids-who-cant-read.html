A game for kids who can't read yet: PC games without text

Four-year-olds often want to play on the laptop like the grown-ups, but cannot read instructions yet. Many "learning games" assume letters or spoken explanations. This helps you choose.

How a game works without reading

  • Pictures instead of words. Everything important is visible: what is missing, where it belongs, what happens next.
  • Direct dragging and tapping. Dragging a part into place with mouse or touchscreen is easier than using menus.
  • Instant feedback. Sounds and movement show what happened, without judging.
  • No menu mazes. Few buttons, big areas, and a parents' area for anything text-heavy.

What children can practise

Fitting, dragging and painting quietly train hand-eye coordination, looking closely and trying things out. No learning outcome can be promised; it is mainly play, and that is perfectly fine.

Start together

Sit in the first time. Show how to drag or tap, then let your child try. More on choosing is in our checklist for ad-free kids' games.

An example: Little Fixer (Kleiner Reparierer)

In Little Fixer, for ages 3 to 6, everything works without reading: the child finds the right part, drags it into place, screws it tight, scrubs and paints it. Wrong parts just do something funny. Only the parents' area contains text (in 34 languages). 5 repairs are free.

FAQ

Which games suit children who cannot read?

Games that explain themselves through pictures, sounds and direct dragging or tapping, with no text tasks and no time pressure.

Do I need to be there?

Company helps the first time. After that, parents decide depending on the child and the game.
