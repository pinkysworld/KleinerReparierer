Child won't stop playing: making switching off easier

Many parents know it: the game has to end, and the child cries. That is normal for small children, who cannot control impulses well yet. A little preparation often calms things down. This is not parenting counselling, just everyday ideas.

Ideas that often help

  • Agree beforehand. When does it start, for how long, what comes after? Clear routines lower the risk of arguments, guides say.
  • Give a warning. "One more repair, then we are done" is more tangible than "five more minutes".
  • Close with a ritual. For example say goodnight together or put the device in its usual place.
  • Something nice afterwards. A walk, a story or a snack eases the transition.
  • Stay calm, stay consistent. Giving in to whining tends to reinforce it. Tears when stopping are allowed.
  • Pick games with a built-in ending. If the game itself is "done", you do not have to be the spoilsport.

How long may my child play?

Recommendations exist from bodies such as klicksafe or health education agencies. We deliberately set no fixed times; read the current guidance and decide what suits your child. If you are worried, ask your paediatrician.

FAQ

Is it normal for my child to cry when I switch off?

It is common in small children. A warning, steady routines and a ritual can help.

Does a game with a built-in ending help?

It can, because the game itself sets the stopping point. It cannot be guaranteed.
